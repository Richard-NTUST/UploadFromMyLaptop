# Future Work and Recommendations: Towards Energy-Efficient Scheduling

## 1. The Energy Efficiency Limit of Current O-RAN
Our measurements (`02_Results_and_Comparison`) and Code Analysis (`StudyNotes/2026-02-03_srsRAN-Scheduler-Deep-Dive`) have identified a critical bottleneck:

> **The current O-RAN software stack prioritizes Frequency Domain Multiplexing (FDM), spreading user traffic across time. This prevents the O-RU hardware from entering sleep states.**

Even at low traffic loads (e.g., 30%), the "Active-Idle" power remains high because the PA bias and digital front-end must remain active to service sparse symbols.

## 2. Proposed Solution: Time Domain Multiplexing (TDM) / "Bursting"
We recommend modifying the MAC scheduler to clamp the number of UEs per slot ($\le 1$) and allocate full bandwidth to the active user. This creates "Busy Slots" (100% Power) and "Idle Slots" (Sleep Power), significantly reducing the average energy per bit.

### 2.2 Empirical Validation (Proof of Concept)
To validate this hypothesis before code modification, we conducted a "Burst vs. Smooth" experiment on the testbed using `iperf3` to mimic traffic patterns.

- **Smooth Mode (FDM Proxy):** Constant 30% bandwidth usage.
- **Burst Mode (TDM Proxy):** 100% bandwidth usage for 30% of the time, followed by sleep.

**Result:** The platform power dropped from **21.78 W** (Smooth) to **11.25 W** (Burst), a **48% reduction**. This confirms that "racing to sleep" is a viable strategy even on general-purpose CPUs.

#### Quantified Impact (at 30% Load)
| Domain | Current (FDM) | Proposed (TDM) | Saving | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Software Proxy** | ~25.2 W | ~11.9 W | **~13.3 W** | CPU enters C-States (C6/C7) during idle slots. |
| **Hardware O-RU** | ~297 W | ~194 W | **~103 W** | PA enters Micro-Sleep during idle slots. |

## 3. Implementation Roadmap
To realize these savings, the following work package is recommended for the TEEP Stage 2 (Main Internship):

### Phase 1: Software Implementation
1.  **Modify srsRAN Scheduler:** Implement the "UE Clamp" logic in `intra_slice_scheduler.cpp` (as identified in the Deep Dive).
2.  **Verify Burstiness:** Use Wireshark/pcap to confirm that Downlink packets are arriving in contiguous bursts rather than spread flows.

### Phase 2: Hardware-in-Loop Validation
1.  **Acquire O-RU:** Deploy a physical O-RU (e.g., Foxconn RPQN series or Benetel).
2.  **Instrument Power:** Connect a high-speed power analyzer (Yokogawa/Keysight) to the O-RU DC input.
3.  **Measure Micro-Sleep:** Verify if the physical hardware actually enters sleep mode during the 0.5ms idle slots generated by the TDM scheduler.

## 4. Conclusion
The "Gap" between our Software measurements and the Hardware reality is not just an error—it is the **opportunity**. By aligning software scheduling (TDM) with hardware capabilities (Micro-Sleep), we can recover the ~100W of static overhead typically lost in low-load cellular networks.
